> "...it did not take him long to learn the various gambits and ripostes of this non-physical, but nevertheless no-holds-barred, political mayhem."
> -- E.E. "Doc" Smith, "First Lensman"

A subsystem for doing more detailed social conflicts in Fabula Ultima.

Why this? Well...

The goal of this system is to bring Fabula Ultima's usual tactical richness to social conflicts,
rather than representing them as a relatively flat clock.
Many more characters have something to do in a fight than have something that does something with clocks.
At the same time, clocks ought to have a place in larger conflicts, so why not create a system that permits both?

# Damage and Healing

Social Conflicts are resolved through the Fabula Ultima conflict system,
with the following changes and additions.

## Damage

Damage is taken against a character's HP, as with physical conflicts.
Sticks and stones may break bones, but words do hurt, in games as well as the real world.
Characters may heal in the usual ways.

## Affinities

Characters (PCs and NPCs) have four new Affinities:

**Ethos** is the persuasive power of authority or credibility.
Members of a queen's court, a church, or a large guild are Vulnerable to Ethos.
Rebels, outcasts, iconoclasts, and so on would be Resistant to Ethos.

**Pathos** is the appeal to feelings to create sympathy in an audience.
People motivated by instinct, empathy, or passion are Vulnerable to Pathos.
Stoic, toughened, or cold-hearted characters are Resistant to Pathos.

**Logos** is the use of logic and reason to make an argument.
Scholars, thinkers, academics, and so on are Vulnerable to Logos.
Impulsive people, folks suspicious of manipulation, and perfidious people are Resistant to Logos.

**Kairos** is the seeking and striking of vulnerable points at key moments.
People with something to hide, or people who lack conviction, are Vulnerable to Kairos.
People with solid plans, self-control, or strong integrity are Resistant to Kairos.

Before entering your first social conflict,
you should identify one Affinity you'd be Vulnerable to, and one you'd be Resistant to.
Look at your character's Identity, Theme, and Classes for ideas.
For example, a Loremaster might be Vulnerable to Logos because they favor arguments with logical foundations.

Your Affinities will remain fixed until the next time you change Bonds or elements of your Theme.
If the change would logically lead to your Affinities changing - such as a formerly peaceful
character taking a bond of Hate toward an enemy - then you may rearrange your Affinities to match.

The GM may assign social Affinities to NPCs as appropriate.
There is no guarantee that any NPC will have only one Vulnerability or Resistance.
Certain NPCs might have Immunity or even Absorbtion toward certain Affinities.
For example, a soulless automaton might be entirely Immune to Pathos,
but Vulnerable to a hierarchical command structure (Ethos) or logical arguments (Logos).

# Social Weapons and Armor

When entering a social conflict, characters choose to arm themselves with a specific social weapon and shield.
Characters may use the **Equipment** action to change tactics mid-conflict.

Social weapons are rhetorical strategies or methods of persuasion.
Example social weapons are listed below.

Characters defend against social attacks with either INS (referred to as I.DEF) or WLP (referred to as W.DEF).
Characters may equip a single element of Identity (e.g. a Theme), or a single Bond, as a "shield".
If the chosen element is relative to the social conflict, it adds +2 to both I.DEF and W.DEF.

NPCs may add +1 to I.DEF and W.DEF if one or more Traits would let them resist a certain line of attack.
If a PC knows the NPC's Traits, they can attempt to work around those to neutralize this bonus.
For example, if an enemy has the "haughty" trait, and the PCs find a way to embarrass or humiliate them,
the defense bonus from haughty might go away.

There is no concept of "social armor" or "social accessories".

Equipping a social weapon or shield does not mean a character isn't still carrying their normal equipment.
Qualities of a character's physical equipment still work if appropriate.
For example, if a piece of armor benefited a Weaponmaster's Counterattack skill,
a verbal Counterattack in social conflict would gain the same benefit.

## Sample Social Weapons

Social weapons are the ways you attempt to persuade adversaries in social conflict.
Several examples are given here, but the GM may create others, or approve other players' creations.
GMs should be especially permissive if a given PC's backstory, personality, or actions
suggest a unique social weapon.

- **Bellowing Browbeat**: [MIG + WLP]+1 vs. I.DEF, [HR+4] Kairos. Your presence and voice speak for you, interrupting or dominating the conversation at key moments.
- **Call for Cooperation**: [INS + INS] vs. W.DEF, [HR+6] Ethos. You work to find common ground with your opponent.
- **Cutting Brevity**: [DEX + WLP] vs. W.DEF, [HR+6] Kairos. A few key words can undercut minutes of rhetoric, but your timing must be perfect.
- **Defensive Dissection**: [WLP + WLP] vs. I.DEF, [HR+6] Logos. You tear apart your opponent's arguments and dismantle their rhetoric, revealing their weakness.
- **Fiery Rhetoric**: [INS + WLP]+1 vs. W.DEF, [HR+4] Pathos. You pound the table, making powerful invocations and assertions with the confidence of a preacher.
- **Impassioned Appeal**: [WLP + WLP] vs. W.DEF, [HR+6] Pathos. You bare your soul to your audience, asking for their understanding of your plight.
- **Methodical Indictment**: [INS + INS] vs. W.DEF, [HR+6] Logos. You dismantle your opponent with precise arguments and damning revelations.
- **Patriotic Plea**: [WLP + WLP] vs. W.DEF, [HR+6] Ethos. You invite your target and an audience to consider their loyalties.
- **Scholarly Airs**: [INS + INS] vs. I.DEF, [HR+8] Logos. You engage in palaver, demonstrating an articulate and eloquent elocution.
- **Showy Performance**: [DEX + INS] vs. I.DEF, [HR+6] Pathos. Your flourishes, gestures, posture, and movement contribute just as much as your words.
- **Verbal Judo**: [INS + WLP]+1 vs. I.DEF, [HR+4] Kairos. You're able to turn your opponent's words against them, then press into the opening you create.

## Rare Social Weapons

It's possible for a character to come across something especially useful in social conflicts.
These can function as social weapons even if they're physical in nature.
Examples:

- **Damning Documents**: [INS + INS] vs. W.DEF, [HR+8] Logos.
  Evidence of wrongdoing by someone with wealth and power.  
  *Quality*: Does 5 extra damage if you are targeting that individual in social conflicts.
  This social weapon does no damage against characters totally unconnected with the matter in question.
- **Demon's True Name**: [INS + INS]+1 vs. W.DEF, no damage (but see Quality).  
  *Quality*: this weapon does [HR+15] Ethos but only against the particular Demon whose name you know,
  and only one person in a social conflict can wield this.
  If the Demon is defeated, they become a faithful, but not particularly loyal, servitor to the speaker.
- **Signet Ring**: [WLP + WLP] vs. I.DEF, [HR+8] Ethos.
  A token from a monarch, a pope, or someone else with legitimate authority,
  which you can present as evidence you act in their name.
- **Necromancer's Sigil**: Social shield. +2 to I.DEF and W.DEF, but only against Undead social attacks.  
  _This could be repurposed into a Crown of Ivy for Plants, a Demon King's Scepter for Demons,
  or similar items depending on your goals._  
  *Quality*: When carried, all conflicts containing only Undead species default to social conflicts,
  as the creatures will refuse to physically hurt the bearer or their party unless attacked first.
  You may communicate with Undead creatures regardless of their normal intelligence.

Even outside social conflicts, rare social weapons can have their uses.
For example, the Damning Documents might be enough to make the Parliamentary guards move out of the way and admit you during session.
Of course, if those guards are loyal to the wrong master, it may provoke a physical conflict...

Researching a particular social weapon could be handled as Forging a new weapon ("Natural Fantasy").
This might require a character to have levels in Orator or some similar class.
For example, the GM might award the party a rare item that can make the Necromancer's Sigil,
but require the Necromancer PC to Forge it into the final item.

# Rules During Conflicts

## Actions

Most combat actions work as normal.
You **attack** with a social weapon to go on the rhetorical offensive.
You can **hinder** to undercut someone's position or shake them up.
You can **guard** to stand firm against an enemy's attacks, or speak up on someone else's behalf.

Actually using offensive spells or real weapons moves the conflict to a physical conflict.
The use of non-offensive spells or inventory items may be noticed, and could change the direction of the conflict at the GM's discretion.

## Objectives

Sometimes you may want to do more than just beat down an enemy (verbally, that is).
The GM should treat this as a clock, gauging the relative difficulty as a number of segments.
Objective actions will make progress on the clock.
The GM must also decide whether making progress is an opposed check,
and whether an adversary can undo progress in some fashion.

The GM should tell players ahead of time if a given adversary has any extra attainable goals,
over and above simply defeating them. Examples:

- Extracting a confession from a corrupt clergyman or wealthy merchant
- Obtaining an item in the adversary's presence while they're distracted with the conversation
- Learning a demon's true name

## Class Skills

Try to charitably interpret class skills in the context of social conflict wherever possible.
For example, a Weaponmaster might use Bone Crusher to inflict status effects and damage Mind Points, just verbally.
The player must still describe the maneuver! 

If a class skill, item quality, etc. would interact with the traits of a social weapon,
such as whether it's a Melee vs. Ranged weapon, the GM should be permissive in allowing the rule to proceed.
For example, you might allow a Sharpshooter's Crossfire to serve as a quick counter-comment to sarcastically undercut a verbal attack.

## Opportunities

Most opportunities work as well in social conflict as in other scenarios.
If you want to have certain opportunities have more concrete outcomes, here's some examples of how.

- **Bonding**: if a character didn't already have a social shield, a Bond created in the moment can grant one.
- **Faux Pas**: a compromising statement can also mark progress on a clock meant to expose an adversary.
- **Favor**: characters can treat such favors as rare loot (see "Defeat" below) which might function as social weapons.
- **Information**: information might justify a one-time damage boost, if the character can find a way to make it part of their attack.
- **Plot Twist!**: the outcome of a social conflict can be very different depending on the audience!
  A corrupt clergyman's confession might matter far more if a senior Church officer is on hand to hear it.
- **Unmask**: social conflicts aren't just about learning something, but enacting change.
  Knowing these things about an adversary can help steer the conflict, but wouldn't end it.

A special outcome for certain Opportunities such as Faux Pas or Unmask would be to change an adversary's Affinities.
For example, a heartless person might be rattled enough by their own admissions to lose their Resistance to Pathos -
maybe for the next turn, maybe for the encounter!

## Defeat

What are some possible outcomes of defeating an adversary in social conflicts?

- Extracting a concession on a specific point, e.g. a city council agreeing to send food and medicine to remote villages
- Granting a difficult request, e.g. a sentinel letting adventurers through a locked gate
- Changing position on a particular issue, e.g. a king deciding that going to war is the right move
- Changing attitudes, e.g. replacing "haughty" with "humble" as a proud wizard's Trait

Regardless of the specifics, a social defeat should always mean _something_!
It's not enough to simply have the adversary walk away, unless that was the PCs' actual goal.

Defeated adversaries can offer loot as normal.
This might be an item (if that's what the PCs wanted from the conflict),
but it could also be secret knowledge, a promise, or something more intangible.
These intangibles might even function as rare social weapons.

PC Surrender, likewise, may mean a concession from the PC.
If a character is an authority figure, it _might_ mean them being hauled away in chains -
although it would be a far more civilized matter for the other PCs to free them.
And of course, a PC changing Theme or Bonds is very appropriate for tense social situations.

# Adversaries

In general, creating social adversaries works the same way as for physical conflicts.
The additional steps are:

- Pick social Affinities (based on Traits, rather than Species)
- Pick social Skills in place of (or in addition to) other Skills

Many existing Skills can be adapted to social conflict with little effort.
For example, an adversary with the "Crisis Effect" skill might dig in if pushed too far,
changing their Affinities to become hardened against both emotional and logical arguments.
Some Skills can conceivably be used in both modes.

If you intend to let an adversary engage in both physical and social conflict, you'll want two sets of Skills, one for each mode.

## Specific Social Skills

**Damage Resistance**: this Skill only provides Resistance to one social Affinity.

**Final Act**: a defeated adversary might reveal some awful secret or truth, harming everyone who hears it.

**Improved Defenses**: the adversary would improve their INS and/or WLP defenses.

**Special Attack**: rather than controlling or petrifying a PC, a Special Attack might cause them to lose or change their action.
An adversary might bring up an especially devastating point or some incriminating evidence - for example, did the PCs try to peacefully
negotiate with an enemy earlier? - that could start a clock which must be progressed to "free" a character.

**Spellcaster**: the GM can treat NPC Spells as being social in nature, e.g. "Cursed Breath" might be a fiery speech
that does no physical harm, but does cause HP loss and inflict a status.

## Example Adversary: The Angry Mob

A mob has gathered, ready to attack someone.
This could be due to fears of a werewolf's curse, suspicion of someone from a race not usually found in the region,
accusations of theft, or whatever! No matter what the reason, the mob must be calmed, lest someone get hurt.

**The Angry Mob** -- Lv 5 -- Humanoid, Champion (2)  
*Traits*: angry, incoherent  
DEX d8, INS d6, MIG d10, WLP d8 -- HP 140 (Crisis 70) -- MP 100 -- Init 9  
I.DEF 10 -- W.DEF 8 -- Ethos (Vulnerable), Pathos (Vulnerable), Logos (Immune), Kairos (Resistant)

Social Attacks:
- **Discordant Shouting**: [WLP+WLP] vs. W.DEF. [HR+6] Pathos. Multi(2).
- **Terrifying Unpredictability**: [MIG+MIG] vs. I.DEF. [HR+6] Kairos. Multi(2).

Special Rules:
- **Angrier But Wearier**: When the Mob enters Crisis, it gains Absorbtion to Pathos and becomes Resistant to Logos.

## Example Adversary: Cardinal Mallus

The Church is said to be corrupt, and this reputation is largely due to the deeds of people such as Cardinal Mallus.
He is officially a member of the church and must be seen to obey the dictates of its hierarchy.
In this capacity, he holds great power over his territory.
In secret, his secret treasure vault and wealth of blackmail materials give him vast powers over the population.
He knows enough demonology to summon succubi - demonic seducers.
They cannot heal the heartache of his first love running into the arms of the church's knight-captain, but they aid him in other ways.

**Cardinal Mallus** -- Lv 20 -- Humanoid, Champion (3), Villain (5)  
*Traits*: corrupt, hedonistic, refined, diabolical  
DEX d8, INS d10, MIG d6, WLP d12 -- HP 210 (Crisis 105) -- MP 180 -- Init 12  
I.DEF 12 -- W.DEF 13 -- Ethos (Vulnerable), Pathos (), Logos (), Kairos (Resistant)

Social Attacks:
- **In God's Name**: [WLP+WLP]+2 vs. W.DEF. [HR+10] Ethos. Mallus speaks for the Church, and none can gainsay God.
- **Hellish Hypnosis**: [INS+INS]+2 vs. I.DEF. [HR+10] Logos. Quiet. Measured. Reserved. Deadly.
  If the target is **dazed**, the Cardinal may dictate their next action.
- **Tempter's Tongue**: [INS+WLP]+2 vs. W.DEF. [HR+10] Pathos.
  Mallus may discern whatever a PC holds dear, then coolly make a threat against it.
  An adventurer may choose to create a Bond of Hatred toward Mallus, if they do not already haver one.
  If they create a Bond, they take no damage.
  A character who already has this Bond takes damage as normal.

Other Actions:
- **Demonic Servitor**: Mallus may conjure a succubus as a reinforcement, paying 10 MP per succubus.
  Mallus is limited to ever summoning a total of 3 succubi per scene, but may summon more than one in a single action.
  Succubi are a soldier-type Demon adversary, who will attempt to seduce or befuddle PCs.
  Such reinforcements will appear to be human, but could conceivably be unmasked if a PC has the means to do so.
- **Soul Mark**: Mallus may apply one of his Soul Mark effects (below) to an enemy. An enemy may only have one Soul Mark effect at a time.
  Mark effects end when the conflict ends. Effects that would remove a status effect may instead be used to remove a mark.
- **Unexpected Ally**: Mallus may spend an action and spend 1 Ultima Point to choose a non-hostile NPC on the scene.
  If he does, that NPC becomes helpful to him so long as he is kind and respectful and his requests are reasonable.

Spells:
- **Recitation of Scripture**: 10 MP, One creature, Instantaneous. The target suffers **dazed** and **shaken**.
- **Pose of Sanctity**: 10 MP, Self, Instantaneous. Mallus heals 40 HP.

Magic Check: [INS+WLP]+2

Special Rules:
- **Pain's Banquet**: if attacked by a creature who has a Bond of Hatred toward him, Mallus regains 10 MP.
- When Mallus enters Crisis, he can no longer maintain his holy pose, and his darker nature takes over.
  His Affinities change as follows: Ethos (Resistant), Pathos (Vulnerable), Logos (Resistant), Kairos (-).
- Mallus may equip and use adventuring equipment. He ordinarily equips the **Necronomicon**.

Soul Marks:
- **Mark of Despair**: the next time a marked creature recovers HP or MP, they only recover half as much.
- **Mark of Destiny**: Mallus may force a marked creature to reroll both dice on any check. He may only affect a creature like this once per scene.
- **Mark of Forbiddance**: if the marked creature performs the Guard action, they lose 10 HP and 10 MP.
- **Mark of Mortality**: none of the marked creature's attribute die sizes may rise above their natural values.
  Effects that would boost such attribute dice are suspended until the mark ends.

Loot:
- **Forbidden Knowledge**. Mallus may offer characters dark wisdom and otherworldly secrets if defeated.
  This knowledge may function as a rare item usable in Rituals, Projects, and the like.
- The **Necronomicon** (Fabula Ultima p. 270). He may drop it if forced to flee, or offer it as temptation to a spellcasting character.
- **Damning Documents** (above). Characters who subtly search the area while Cardinal Mallus engages with others may have come across these.
  They implicate the Cardinal and could be used to hasten his downfall.

Characters with the Damning Documents or similarly persuasive evidence may denounce Mallus before an audience by defeating him in social conflict.
The effects of this largely depend on who was in the audience at the time.
It could lead to Mallus being led away in chains - or the beginning of a physical battle with a powerful demonologist!

Characters might also try to bargain with a level 20 or higher Demon to revoke Mallus' power.
In this case, Mallus will lose his **Demonic Servitor** and **Soul Mark** abilities,
and any physical conflict would leave him deprived of other similar abilities.
Convincing such a Demon to act against a loyal and powerful ally would no doubt be a social conflict all on its own...

## Example Adversary: Goldscale

Goldscale is an ancient dragon. Greedy beyond measure, she coiled herself around the elves' world tree
and demanded tribute from any elf that wished to renew their immortality via their yearly pilgrimage to the tree.
She receives visitors and will haughtily engage in discourse with them, arrogantly believing herself above all mortal threats.
Mortals might entreat Goldscale for some of her gathered treasure - or might slyly try to draw out the secret of her vulnerability.

**Goldscale** - Lv 40 - Monster, Champion (4), Villain (10)  
*Traits*: greedy, arrogant, ancient, talkative  
DEX d8, INS d8, MIG d10, WLP d10 - HP 520 (Crisis 260) - MP 180 - Init 12  
I.DEF 9 - W.DEF 12 - Ethos (Resistant), Pathos (-), Logos (Vulnerable), Kairos (-)

Social Attacks:
- **Cunning Colloquy**: [DEX+INS]+4 vs. W.DEF, [HR+20] Pathos. An almost purring series of boasts and gloating, all laden with menace. This attack has multi(2).
- **Raging Rake**: [MIG+WLP]+4 vs. W.DEF, [HR+25] Kairos. A sudden swipe of the claws to reinforce a bellowed declaration of power.
- **Mocking Temptation**: [WLP+WLP]+4 vs. W.DEF, [HR+15] Pathos. Goldscale taunts her enemies with her power and knowledge,
  seemingly promising them what they most desire. This attack inflicts **shaken**.

Spells:
- **Beguiling Hospitality**: 20 MP, Special, Instantaneous. Choose any number of creatures you can see: each of them suffers **dazed**.
- **Gesture of Befuddlement**: 10 MP, One creature, Instantaneous. The target suffers [HR+25] Pathos damage and suffers **slow**.
- **Rhetorical Reverse**: 10 MP, One creature, Instantaneous. The target suffers [HR+25] Kairos damage. Then, you recover an amount of HP equal to half the HP loss they suffered.
- **Venomous Words**: 10xT MP, Up to three creatures, Instantaneous. Each target hit by this spell suffers **poisoned**.

Magic Check: [INS+WLP]+4

Other Actions:
- **Font of Knowledge**: Gloating at her knowledge of ancient lore, Goldscale recovers 10 MP when she takes Logos damage.
  Only available when not in Crisis.
- **"Do Not Mock Me!"**: Goldscale rages at the temerity of mortals to engage with her.
  If she takes Logos damage, Goldscale will make a free immediate counterattack, treating the HR as 0 for damage purposes.
  Only available when in Crisis.
- **All-Seeing Eye**: Goldscale has great discernment and a magical gift of insight.
  When she attacks with **Mocking Temptation**, she may spend 10 MP to make the offer personal.
  If she does so, she may recover HP equal half the amount she inflicted.

Special Rules:
- Goldscale is immune to the **shaken** and **weak** status effects.
- When Goldscale enters Crisis, she becomes enraged, losing her pose of intellect and exposing her nature as a prideful, instinct-driven beast.
  Her Affinities change to Ethos (Resistant), Pathos (Vulnerable), Logos (Resistant), Kairos (Vulnerable).
  She loses access to **Font of Knowledge** and unlocks **"Do Not Mock Me!"**
- If Goldscale is defeated in social conflict, she will angrily eject the party and forbid them re-entry to her sanctum.
  This will inflict 20 points of untyped damage and inflict the **shaken** status on everyone.

Loot:
- **Everseeds**: as Goldscale recited her glorious past deeds, your party secretly harvested spores from around the roots of the world tree.
  These can be used as rare items, such as creating the arrow that will melt Goldscale's otherwise-impenetrable armor - or as a bargaining chip
  to peacefully settle negotiations between elves and their hostile neighbors.
- **Hidden Lore**: some piece of knowledge Goldscale unthinkingly revealed that can be put to use later.
  It can function as a rare item, enabling a magical creation or forming the basis for a ritual.
