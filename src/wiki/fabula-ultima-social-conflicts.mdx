A subsystem for doing more detailed social conflicts in Fabula Ultima.

# Damage and Healing

Damage is taken against a character's HP. These aren't properly wounds or injuries, but may still be treated as stressful.
Healing works as normal.

## Affinities

Characters (PCs and NPCs) have four new Affinities:

**Ethos** is the persuasive power of authority or credibility.
Members of a queen's court, a church, or a large guild are Vulnerable to Ethos.
Rebels, outcasts, iconoclasts, and so on would be Resistant to Ethos.

**Pathos** is the appeal to feelings to create sympathy in an audience.
People motivated by instinct, empathy, or passion are Vulnerable to Pathos.
Stoic, toughened, or cold-hearted characters are Resistant to Pathos.

**Logos** is the use of logic and reason to make an argument.
Scholars, thinkers, academics, and so on are Vulnerable to Logos.
Impulsive people, folks suspicious of manipulation, and perfidious people are Resistant to Logos.

**Kairos** is the seeking and striking of vulnerable points at key moments.
People with something to hide, or people who lack conviction, are Vulnerable to Kairos.
People with solid plans, self-control, or strong integrity are Resistant to Kairos.

Before entering your first social conflict,
you should identify one Affinity you'd be Vulnerable to, and one you'd be Resistant to.
Look at your character's Identity, Theme, and Classes for ideas.
For example, a Loremaster might be Vulnerable to Logos because they favor arguments with logical foundations.
Thereafter, these Affinities will remain as they are.
Changes to your Bonds or Themes might trigger a change in Affinities, at the GM's discretion.

The GM may assign social Affinities to NPCs as appropriate.
There is no guarantee that any NPC will have only one Vulnerability or Resistance, for example.
Certain NPCs - such as emotionless robots - may be Immune to things like Pathos!

# Social Weapons and Armor

When entering a social conflict, characters choose to arm themselves with social weapons, taken from the list below.
Characters may use the **Equipment** action to change tactics.

Characters may also equip a single element of Identity (e.g. a Theme), or a single Bond, as a "shield".
If the chosen element is relative to the social conflict, it adds +2 to their defenses.

NPCs may add +1 to their Defenses if one or more Traits would let them resist an attack.

## Sample Social Weapons

* **Bellowing Browbeat**: [MIG + WLP]+1 vs. INS, [HR+4] Kairos. Your presence and voice speak for you, interrupting or dominating the conversation at key moments.
* **Call for Cooperation**: [INS + INS] vs. WLP, [HR+6] Ethos. You work to find common ground with your opponent.
* **Cutting Brevity**: [DEX + WLP] vs. WLP, [HR+6] Kairos. A few key words can undercut minutes of rhetoric, but your timing must be perfect.
* **Defensive Dissection**: [WLP + WLP] vs. INS, [HR+6] Logos. You tear apart your opponent's arguments and dismantle their rhetoric, revealing their weakness.
* **Fiery Rhetoric**: [INS + WLP]+1 vs. WLP, [HR+4] Pathos. You pound the table, making powerful invocations and assertions with the confidence of a preacher.
* **Impassioned Appeal**: [WLP + WLP] vs. WLP, [HR+6] Pathos. You bare your soul to your audience, asking for their understanding of your plight.
* **Methodical Indictment**: [INS + INS] vs. WLP, [HR+6] Logos. You dismantle your opponent with precise arguments and damning revelations.
* **Patriotic Plea**: [WLP + WLP] vs. WLP, [HR+6] Ethos. You invite your target and an audience to consider their loyalties.
* **Showy Performance**: [DEX + INS] vs. INS, [HR+6] Pathos. Your flourishes, gestures, posture, and movement contribute just as much as your words.
* **Verbal Judo**: [INS + WLP]+1 vs. INS, [HR+4] Kairos. You're able to turn your opponent's words against them, then press into the opening you create.

The GM should be permissive in letting PCs propose other social weapons.

## Rare Social Weapons

It's possible for a character to come across something that specially equips them for a later social engagement.
Example:

- **Damning Documents**: [INS + INS] vs. WLP, [HR+8] Logos. Evidence of wrongdoing by someone in authority. Does 5 extra damage if you are targeting that individual.
- **Signet Ring**: [WLP + WLP] vs. INS, [HR+8] Ethos. A token from someone in power, which you can present as evidence you act with their authority.

Researching a particular social strategy against a specific target could be handled as Forging a new weapon ("Natural Fantasy").
This might require a character to have levels in Orator or some similar class.

# Actions in Combat

Most combat actions work as expected.
You **attack** with a social weapon to go on the rhetorical offensive.
You can **hinder** to undercut someone's position or shake them up.
You can **guard** to stand firm against an enemy's attacks, or speak up on someone else's behalf.

Actually using offensive spells or real weapons moves the conflict to a physical conflict.
The use of non-offensive spells or inventory items may be noticed, and could change the direction of the conflict at the GM's discretion.

## Objective

Sometimes you may want to do more than just beat down an enemy (verbally, that is).
For example, a particular character may want more than just turning the crowd against the Cardinal.
They might want to elicit a confession!
The GM should treat this as a clock, gauging the relative difficulty as a number of segments.
Objective actions will make progress on the clock.
The GM must also decide whether making progress is an opposed check,
and whether an adversary can undo progress in some fashion.

## Class Skills

Try to charitably interpret class skills in the context of social conflict wherever possible.
For example, a Weaponmaster might use Bone Crusher to inflict status effects and damage Mind Points, just verbally.
The player must still describe the maneuver! 

If a class skill, item quality, etc. would interact with the traits of a social weapon,
such as whether it's a Melee vs. Ranged weapon, the GM should be permissive in allowing the rule to proceed.
For example, you might allow a Sharpshooter's Crossfire to serve as a quick counter-comment to sarcastically undercut a verbal attack.

# Adversaries

In general, creating social adversaries works the same way as for physical conflicts.
The additional steps are:

- Pick social Affinities (based on Traits, rather than Species)
- Pick social Skills in place of (or in addition to) other Skills

Many existing Skills can be adapted to social conflict with little effort.
For example, an adversary with the "Crisis Effect" skill might dig in if pushed too far,
changing their Affinities to become hardened against both emotional and logical arguments.
Some Skills can conceivably be used in both modes.

If you intend to let an adversary engage in both physical and social conflict, you'll want two sets of Skills, one for each mode.

## Specific Social Skills

**Final Act**: a defeated adversary might reveal some awful secret or truth, harming everyone who hears it.

**Improved Defenses**: the adversary would improve their INS and/or WLP defenses.

**Special Attack**: rather than controlling or petrifying a PC, a Special Attack might cause them to lose or change their action.
An adversary might bring up an especially devastating point or some incriminating evidence - for example, did the PCs try to peacefully
negotiate with an enemy earlier? - that could start a clock which must be progressed to "free" a character.

**Spellcaster**: the GM can treat NPC Spells as being social in nature, e.g. "Cursed Breath" might be a fiery speech
that does no physical harm, but does cause HP loss and inflict a status.
